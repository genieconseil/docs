Structural analysis determines the internal forces (moments, shears, axial loads) in structural members under applied loads, ensuring adequate strength and stiffness throughout the design life of the structure.

## Load Types (NBCC 2020)

The National Building Code of Canada (NBCC) defines the following design loads:

- **Dead load D**: self-weight of structural elements, superimposed dead loads (topping, finishes, MEP equipment)
- **Live load L**: occupancy loads — floors (1.9-4.8 kPa depending on use), roofs (1.0 kPa minimum)
- **Snow load S**: `S = Is·[Ss·(Cb·Cw·Cs·Ca) + Sr]`; ground snow load Ss varies by location (0.5-7.0 kPa in Canada)
- **Wind load W**: reference velocity pressure `q = 0.5·ρ·V²`; exposure factor, topographic factor, and gust effect factor applied
- **Seismic load E**: design spectral response acceleration Sa(T) from NBCC Appendix C maps; site class modification

### ULS Load Combinations
The governing combination depends on loading scenario:

`1.4D`

`1.25D + 1.5L + 0.5S` (or 0.5W)

`1.25D + 1.5S + 0.5L` (or 0.5W)

`1.0D ± 1.0E + 0.5L + 0.25S` (seismic)

<Note>For counteracting loads (e.g., wind uplift on a roof), use 0.9D as the companion dead load factor to represent the minimum dead load scenario.</Note>

## Analysis Methods

### Linear Static Analysis
Appropriate for regular structures under static or quasi-static loads. Assumes:
- Small deformations
- Linear elastic material behavior
- Equilibrium in the undeformed configuration (first-order theory)

P-delta effects (second-order) must be accounted for in slender structures or when the stability index `Q = ΣPu·Δo / (Vs·hs) > 0.05`.

### Modal Spectral Analysis
Required by NBCC for buildings with irregular configuration or height > 60 m in seismic zones. Extracts natural frequencies and mode shapes, then combines modal contributions using SRSS or CQC method.

### Time-History Analysis
Integrates the equations of motion step-by-step under actual or synthetic ground motion records. Required for base-isolated structures and structures with supplemental damping.

<Tip>For buildings under 60 m in moderate seismic zones, the NBCC equivalent static force procedure is generally sufficient and much faster to apply than dynamic analysis.</Tip>

## Key Beam Formulas

### Moments and Shears

| Loading Case | Max Moment | Max Shear |
|---|---|---|
| Simply supported, UDL w | `wL²/8` (midspan) | `wL/2` (at support) |
| Simply supported, point load P at center | `PL/4` | `P/2` |
| Cantilever, UDL w | `wL²/2` (at root) | `wL` (at root) |
| Fixed-fixed, UDL w | `wL²/12` (at supports), `wL²/24` (midspan) | `wL/2` |

### Deflection Formulas

Simply supported, UDL: `δmax = 5wL⁴ / (384EI)` at midspan

Simply supported, point load at center: `δmax = PL³ / (48EI)`

Cantilever, UDL: `δmax = wL⁴ / (8EI)` at free end

Cantilever, point load at tip: `δmax = PL³ / (3EI)`

## Columns and Buckling

### Euler Critical Load
`Pcr = π²·E·I / (KL)²`

Effective length factor K:
- Pin-pin: K = 1.0
- Fixed-fixed: K = 0.5
- Fixed-free (flagpole): K = 2.0
- Fixed-pin: K = 0.7

Slenderness ratio: `λ = KL/r` where `r = √(I/A)` is the radius of gyration.

Columns with λ > 100-120 are governed by elastic buckling; short columns (λ < 30) by material strength.

### Interaction for Combined Axial + Bending
For members under combined compression and bending, the interaction diagram or interaction formula must be checked:

`Cf/Cr + (Mfx/Mrx + U1·Mfy/Mry) ≤ 1.0` (CSA S16 simplified)

Where U1 is an amplification factor accounting for second-order effects.

<Warning>Always check both in-plane buckling (about weak axis) and lateral-torsional buckling (LTB) for beams and beam-columns. LTB can govern for unbraced long spans and deep sections.</Warning>

## Lateral Force-Resisting Systems

Lateral loads from wind and seismic must be resisted and transmitted to the foundations:

**Shear walls**: reinforced concrete or masonry walls acting in shear; very stiff, efficient for low-to-mid rise buildings

**Braced frames (CBF)**: concentric brace frames — X, K, chevron configurations; economical but less ductile under seismic loading

**Eccentrically braced frames (EBF)**: link element yields in shear before brace buckling; better ductility than CBF

**Moment-resisting frames (SMRF)**: lateral loads resisted by beam-column moment connections; highly ductile for seismic, more flexible (higher drift)

**Core walls**: stair and elevator cores acting as coupled shear walls; standard for high-rise buildings

<Tip>For best seismic performance, combine systems: stiff shear walls or braces at the lower floors for stiffness, and moment frames at upper floors for ductility and redundancy.</Tip>

## Deflection and Drift Limits

| Element | Limit |
|---|---|
| Floors supporting brittle finishes | L/360 |
| Floors, general | L/300 |
| Roofs (avoid ponding) | L/240 |
| Wind inter-story drift | H/400 to H/500 |
| Seismic inter-story drift | 2.5% × hs (NBCC, ductile systems) |
