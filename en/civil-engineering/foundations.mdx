Foundations transfer structural loads to the ground. Adequate foundation design requires understanding soil behavior, geotechnical investigation methods, and the interaction between the structure and the ground.

## Foundation Types

### Shallow Foundations
Shallow foundations bear directly on the soil at relatively low depth (depth-to-width ratio D/B < 1).

- **Spread (isolated) footings**: individual pad under each column; economical when soil is competent
- **Strip (continuous) footings**: beneath load-bearing walls; distributes linear loads
- **Mat (raft) foundations**: a single reinforced concrete slab beneath the entire building footprint; used when soil is weak, loads are high, or differential settlement must be minimized

<Note>Shallow foundations are generally appropriate when the net allowable bearing pressure qnet ≥ 100 kPa and the competent bearing stratum is within 3 m of the surface.</Note>

### Deep Foundations
When near-surface soils are inadequate, deep foundations transfer loads to deeper, more competent strata:

- **Driven piles**: steel H-piles, steel pipe piles, precast concrete piles; installed by hammering, vibratory, or pressing methods
- **Drilled shafts (caissons)**: large-diameter bored piles with concrete cast in place; can support very high loads
- **Micropiles**: diameter < 300 mm; used for retrofit underpinning and restricted-access conditions
- **Helical piles**: steel shaft with helical plates; fast installation, suitable for tension and compression

## Bearing Capacity

### Terzaghi's Bearing Capacity Equation
For a strip footing on homogeneous soil:

`qu = c·Nc + q·Nq + 0.5·γ·B·Nγ`

Where:
- `c` = soil cohesion (kPa)
- `q = γ·D` = effective overburden stress at foundation level
- `γ` = unit weight of soil (kN/m³)
- `B` = footing width (m)
- `Nc, Nq, Nγ` = dimensionless bearing capacity factors (functions of friction angle φ)

Net allowable bearing capacity (FS = 3):

`qallowable = (qu - q) / FS`

### Pile Capacity
Total pile resistance combines skin friction and tip resistance:

`Qu = Qs + Qp = fs·As + qp·Ap`

Where `fs` is the average unit skin friction along the shaft and `qp` is the unit tip resistance.

<Warning>Never reduce the factor of safety below 2.5 for bearing capacity without a comprehensive geotechnical investigation and justified rationale. Bearing capacity failures are sudden and catastrophic.</Warning>

## Soil Investigation

A geotechnical investigation program typically includes:

### Standard Penetration Test (SPT)
A split-spoon sampler is driven 450 mm (18 in) by a 63.5 kg hammer falling 760 mm. The blow count for the last 300 mm is the N-value. Corrections applied:
- `N60` = energy correction (field efficiency to 60% standard)
- `(N1)60` = overburden correction using `CN = (Pa/σ'v)^0.5`

Correlations: N60 → relative density Dr (sands), undrained shear strength Su (clays), friction angle φ.

### Cone Penetration Test (CPT)
A cone is pushed into the ground at a constant rate (2 cm/s). Continuous measurements of tip resistance `qc` and sleeve friction `fs`. The friction ratio `Rf = fs/qc` is used for soil classification.

The CPT is more repeatable than the SPT and provides a continuous soil profile. Preferred for soft clay and loose sand characterization.

### Laboratory Testing
- **Grain size analysis**: sieve and hydrometer tests for soil classification (USCS, AASHTO)
- **Atterberg limits**: plastic limit (PL), liquid limit (LL), plasticity index (PI = LL - PL) for fine-grained soils
- **Consolidation test (oedometer)**: Cc (compression index), Cv (coefficient of consolidation), OCR
- **Shear strength**: direct shear (φ, c), triaxial (UU, CU, CD), vane shear (Su in field)

<Tip>For important projects, combine at least two investigation methods (e.g., SPT + CPT) to cross-validate data and interpolate between boreholes. The investigation budget is typically 0.5-1.5% of construction cost — well spent given the risk of foundation problems.</Tip>

## Settlement Analysis

### Immediate (Elastic) Settlement
`Si = q·B·(1-ν²)·Iw / Es`

Occurs immediately upon load application; governs in sands and stiff clays.

### Primary Consolidation Settlement (Clays)
`Sc = Cc/(1+e0) · H · log[(σ'v0 + Δσ'v)/σ'v0]` (normally consolidated clay)

`Sc = Cs/(1+e0) · H · log[(σ'v0 + Δσ'v)/σ'v0]` (overconsolidated, Δσ' < preconsolidation pressure)

Time rate of consolidation: `Tv = Cv·t / Hdr²` where Hdr is the drainage path length.

### Differential Settlement Limits
Differential settlement is more damaging than uniform settlement:
- Rigid structures (brick, concrete frames): L/600
- Flexible structures (steel frames): L/300
- Foundation tilt: 1/300 for most buildings

<Warning>Differential settlements exceeding L/300 to L/600 can crack finishes, jam doors, and in severe cases compromise structural integrity. Monitor settlements with survey benchmarks during and after construction.</Warning>

## Ground Improvement

When natural soils are inadequate and deep foundations are uneconomical:

- **Dynamic compaction**: repeated drops of heavy weight (10-20 tonnes from 15-20 m); densifies loose granular fills and sands to 6-10 m depth
- **Stone columns (vibro-replacement)**: gravel columns installed by vibratory probe; increase bearing capacity and accelerate consolidation in soft clays
- **Chemical grouting**: injection of cement, silicate, or polyurethane grouts to fill voids and increase stiffness
- **Lime/cement stabilization**: mixing lime or cement into soft clays increases strength and reduces compressibility
- **Prefabricated vertical drains (PVDs)**: accelerate primary consolidation in soft clays by reducing drainage path length
