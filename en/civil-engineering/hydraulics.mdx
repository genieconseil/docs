Hydraulics governs the behavior of water in pipes, channels, and hydraulic structures. Hydrology quantifies rainfall-runoff relationships and peak flows for the design of drainage infrastructure.

## Fundamental Equations

### Continuity Equation
For steady, incompressible flow, conservation of mass requires:

`Q = A₁·V₁ = A₂·V₂`

Where Q is the volumetric flow rate (m³/s), A is the cross-sectional area (m²), and V is the mean velocity (m/s). Velocity increases where the cross-section narrows.

### Bernoulli's Equation
For an ideal (inviscid), incompressible fluid in steady flow:

`P/γ + V²/2g + z = H = constant`

The three terms represent pressure head, velocity head, and elevation head respectively. Their sum is the total hydraulic head H.

**Generalized Bernoulli** (with losses):

`H₁ = H₂ + hf`

Where `hf` represents all head losses between sections 1 and 2.

**Practical applications**:
- Orifice efflux velocity: `V = Cv·√(2g·h)` with `Cv ≈ 0.97-0.99`
- Sharp-crested rectangular weir: `Q = Cd·(2/3)·L·√(2g)·H^(3/2)`, `Cd ≈ 0.62`
- Venturi meter: `Q = Cd·A2·√(2g·Δh/(1-(A2/A1)²))`

<Note>Bernoulli's equation applies along a streamline. For flow between pipe sections, it is usually applied along the pipe centerline as the energy equation.</Note>

## Pipe Flow

### Darcy-Weisbach Equation
The standard method for head loss due to pipe friction:

`hf = f · (L/D) · V²/(2g)`

The Darcy friction factor f comes from the Moody diagram:
- Laminar flow (Re < 2300): `f = 64/Re`
- Turbulent, smooth pipe: Blasius formula `f = 0.316/Re^0.25` (Re < 100,000)
- Turbulent, rough pipe (Colebrook-White): `1/√f = -2·log(ks/(3.7D) + 2.51/(Re·√f))`

Swamee-Jain explicit approximation (avoids iteration):

`f = 0.25 / [log(ks/(3.7D) + 5.74/Re^0.9)]²`

### Hazen-Williams Formula (water only)
Widely used in water distribution system design:

`V = 0.849·C·R^0.63·S^0.54`

`Q = 0.849·C·A·R^0.63·S^0.54`

Typical C values: PVC 150, lined ductile iron 130-140, concrete 120, old unlined iron 80-100.

<Warning>The Hazen-Williams formula is empirical, calibrated for water at typical temperatures, and should not be used for other fluids or for very high or low velocities. Darcy-Weisbach is more rigorous and universally applicable.</Warning>

### Minor (Local) Losses
`hm = K · V²/(2g)`

Common K values: 90° standard elbow 0.9, 90° long-radius elbow 0.4, gate valve (fully open) 0.2, check valve 2.0, pipe entrance (sharp) 0.5, sudden expansion `(1 - A1/A2)²`.

### Pipe Network Analysis
For complex networks: Hardy-Cross iterative method or matrix (node-based) methods. Software: EPANET (free, US EPA), WaterGEMS, InfoWorks WS.

## Open Channel Flow

### Manning's Equation
`V = (1/n) · R^(2/3) · S^(1/2)`   `Q = (1/n) · A · R^(2/3) · S^(1/2)`

Where:
- `n` = Manning's roughness coefficient: concrete 0.012, natural streams 0.025-0.050, riprap 0.025-0.030
- `R = A/P` = hydraulic radius (m); A = flow area, P = wetted perimeter
- `S` = channel bottom slope (m/m)

**Normal depth** `yn`: the depth at which uniform flow occurs for given Q, n, S, and section shape.

**Critical depth** `yc`: depth where Froude number `Fr = V/√(g·D) = 1`; D = hydraulic depth = A/T (T = top water surface width).

- Fr < 1: subcritical flow (tranquil) — typical in most drainage channels
- Fr > 1: supercritical flow (rapid) — downstream of steep chutes
- Hydraulic jump: abrupt transition from supercritical to subcritical; significant energy dissipation

## Storm Water Management

### Rational Method
For small urban catchments (< 80 ha):

`Q = C·i·A / 360`

Where:
- `Q` = peak flow (m³/s)
- `C` = runoff coefficient: 0.20 (parks/lawns), 0.65-0.80 (residential), 0.85-0.95 (commercial/industrial)
- `i` = rainfall intensity (mm/h) for design return period and time of concentration tc
- `A` = drainage area (ha)

**Time of concentration** tc: travel time from the hydraulically most remote point to the outlet. Estimate using Kirpich, Bransby-Williams, or SCS TR-55 method.

**IDF curves** (Intensity-Duration-Frequency): established from regional historical rainfall data. Design return periods: 2-10 years for minor systems (storm sewers), 100 years for major systems (roads, channels).

<Tip>For larger catchments (> 80 ha) or when storage and routing are important, use the SCS (NRCS) curve number method or hydrograph routing methods. Software: SWMM (EPA, free), PCSWMM, HEC-HMS.</Tip>

### Detention Pond Design
Detention ponds reduce peak flows by temporarily storing runoff:

1. Route the design storm hydrograph through the pond
2. Size the outlet structure (weir, orifice, pipe) to limit outflow to the pre-development peak
3. Size the pond volume to contain the difference between inflow and outflow hydrographs
4. Include an emergency spillway for storms exceeding the design event

### Drainage Infrastructure

**Storm sewers**: design for full-pipe flow using Manning's equation; minimum velocity 0.6 m/s (self-cleaning), maximum 3-4 m/s (erosion control). Minimum slope typically 0.5-1.0%.

**Culverts**: inlet-controlled or outlet-controlled depending on tailwater and inlet geometry. HY-8 (FHWA) software widely used.

**Outfall protection**: riprap apron, energy dissipator basin, or plunge pool to prevent scour at channel outlets.
