## What is Nuclear Engineering?

Nuclear engineering applies the principles of nuclear physics and nuclear chemistry to the design, operation, and safety of systems that involve nuclear reactions. The discipline spans a remarkable range of applications — from the generation of baseload electricity with near-zero greenhouse gas emissions, to life-saving medical isotopes, to the management of radioactive materials across their entire lifecycle.

Unlike most engineering disciplines, nuclear engineering must grapple with invisible, ionizing radiation that poses biological hazards not apparent to the senses, with timescales ranging from microseconds (prompt neutron lifetimes) to hundreds of thousands of years (high-level waste isotope half-lives), and with public perception shaped as much by historical events as by technical reality.

## Canada's Unique Position in Nuclear Technology

Canada is one of only a handful of countries that has independently developed and operates its own nuclear reactor technology. This is not a minor technical footnote — it reflects decades of sustained investment in science and engineering that has produced technology now operating in six countries worldwide.

### The CANDU Reactor — A Made-in-Canada Technology

**CANDU** (CANada Deuterium Uranium) reactors were developed by **Atomic Energy of Canada Limited (AECL)**, a federal Crown corporation established in 1952. The technology emerged from Canada's wartime participation in the Manhattan Project and a deliberate post-war decision to pursue peaceful nuclear power using enriched-uranium-free fuel.

**CANDU's defining technical advantages:**

| Feature | CANDU Design | Conventional PWR/BWR |
|---|---|---|
| **Fuel** | Natural uranium (0.7% U-235) | Enriched uranium (3-5% U-235) |
| **Moderator** | Heavy water (D₂O) | Light water (H₂O) |
| **Coolant** | Heavy water (D₂O) | Light water (H₂O) or boiling water |
| **Refueling** | **Online refueling** — while at full power | Shutdown required for refueling |
| **Pressure vessel vs. pressure tubes** | Horizontal pressure tubes — calandria design | Single large pressure vessel |
| **Fuel burnup** | ~7,500 MWd/tU | ~45,000 MWd/tU (enriched) |

**Why natural uranium matters**: The ability to use unenriched natural uranium means CANDU nations do not need uranium enrichment facilities — a significant non-proliferation advantage and a major cost saving (enrichment is expensive). It also means CANDU reactors can operate on a wider variety of fuel types (DUPIC fuel from used PWR fuel, thorium fuel, MOX fuel).

**Online refueling**: Perhaps CANDU's most operationally significant feature. A fueling machine connects to one end of a fuel channel while the reactor operates at full power, pushes fresh fuel bundles in, and catches spent bundles from the other end. This allows CANDU stations to maintain very high capacity factors (>90%) compared to light water reactors that must shut down for refueling every 18-24 months.

## Operating CANDU Reactors in Canada

| Station | Province | Operator | Units | Net Capacity |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Bruce A** | Ontario | Bruce Power | 4 (Units 1-4) | ~3,100 MWe |
| **Bruce B** | Ontario | Bruce Power | 4 (Units 5-8) | ~3,200 MWe |
| **Darlington** | Ontario | OPG | 4 | ~3,500 MWe |
| **Pickering** | Ontario | OPG | 4 (operating) | ~2,000 MWe |
| **Point Lepreau** | New Brunswick | NB Power | 1 | ~660 MWe |

Nuclear power generates approximately **60% of Ontario's electricity** (the highest proportion of nuclear in any North American jurisdiction), making Ontario's grid among the lowest-carbon in the hemisphere. New Brunswick's nuclear capacity provides an important strategic baseload for the province's energy sovereignty.

### Refurbishment Programs

Canada's operating reactors are undergoing major life-extension refurbishments:

- **Darlington Refurbishment** (OPG): 16-year, ~$12.8 billion program to extend Darlington's operating life by 30 years through major component replacement (pressure tubes, calandria tubes, feeders, steam generators, fuel channels)
- **Bruce Power Life Extension**: similar refurbishment of Bruce A and B units — one of the largest nuclear investment programs in Canadian history
- **Point Lepreau**: completed refurbishment in 2012

These refurbishment programs generate enormous demand for nuclear engineers, project managers, radiation protection specialists, and skilled tradespeople.

## Canada's Nuclear Fuel Cycle

### Uranium Mining — Saskatchewan's Athabasca Basin

The **Athabasca Basin** in northern Saskatchewan hosts the highest-grade uranium deposits on Earth:

| Mine | Operator | Grade (avg.) | Status |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Cigar Lake** | Cameco / Orano | ~15% U₃O₈ | Operating — world's highest grade active mine |
| **McArthur River** | Cameco / Orano | ~9% U₃O₈ | Restarted after care-and-maintenance |
| **Key Lake** (mill) | Cameco | — | Processes McArthur River ore |
| **McClean Lake** (mill) | Orano | — | Processes Cigar Lake ore |

For comparison, the world average uranium grade is approximately 0.1% U₃O₈. Athabasca Basin grades are 10-100× higher than the global average, making them economically viable even at uranium prices that would shut down most other mines.

### Uranium Conversion and Fuel Fabrication

After milling to produce uranium oxide concentrate (U₃O₈, "yellowcake"), the Canadian fuel cycle continues:

- **Conversion to UO₂**: At **Cameco's Port Hope** facility (Ontario) — the largest UO₂ conversion facility in the world — uranium trioxide (UO₃) is reduced to uranium dioxide (UO₂) powder for CANDU fuel pellets
- **Fuel fabrication**: UO₂ pellets are sintered and loaded into zirconium alloy (Zircaloy-4) tubes, assembled into CANDU fuel bundles (37-element or 43-element designs) at **Cameco's Port Hope** and **Global Nuclear Fuel Americas** facilities
- **No enrichment step required** — a unique feature of the CANDU cycle

### Spent Fuel Management — The NWMO Deep Geological Repository

Canada's plan for the permanent management of used nuclear fuel is managed by the **Nuclear Waste Management Organization (NWMO)**, established under the *Nuclear Fuel Waste Act* (2002).

The NWMO selected **Ignace, Ontario** (2024) as the site for Canada's deep geological repository (DGR) — a repository in crystalline Shield rock approximately 500 meters below surface that will permanently isolate used CANDU fuel from the environment. The multi-barrier system includes:
1. Ceramic fuel pellets (immobilize radioactivity in stable matrix)
2. Zircaloy fuel sheath (metallic containment)
3. Copper-coated steel used fuel container (corrosion-resistant outer barrier)
4. Bentonite clay buffer (swells when wet, seals gaps, retards groundwater movement)
5. Deep geological formation (stable crystalline rock for millions of years)

## Small Modular Reactors (SMR) — Canada's Next Nuclear Wave

Canada has positioned itself as a global leader in SMR development, with federal and provincial government support creating a remarkable cluster of SMR projects:

### Why Canada for SMRs?

- **Remote communities and mines**: Northern Canada has hundreds of diesel-dependent remote communities and mines. SMRs can provide clean, reliable power without long transmission lines.
- **Industrial decarbonization**: Oil sands operations consume vast amounts of natural gas for steam and heat. High-temperature SMRs could displace this.
- **International export**: CANDU technology has already been deployed in eight countries. Canada wants to repeat this success with SMRs.
- **Existing nuclear workforce and supply chain**: Ontario's nuclear sector provides qualified engineers, tradespeople, and a deep supply chain.

### Active Canadian SMR Projects

| Developer | Technology | Location | Status (2024) |
|---|---|---|---|
| **ARC Clean Technology** | ARC-100 (sodium-cooled fast reactor, 100 MWe) | Point Lepreau, NB | CNSC regulatory pre-licensing; provincial support |
| **Moltex Energy** | SSR-W (stable salt reactor — wasteform, 300 MWe) | Point Lepreau, NB | Design pre-licensing; uses used CANDU fuel |
| **Global First Power** | MMR (micro modular reactor, 15 MWe HTR) | Chalk River, ON | Site preparation; first Canadian SMR to break ground |
| **Terrestrial Energy** | IMSR (integral molten salt reactor, 195 MWe) | Oakville, ON | CNSC regulatory pre-licensing Stage 2 complete |
| **Ultra Safe Nuclear** | MMR Energy System | Whiteshell, MB | Early development |
| **X-energy** | Xe-100 (pebble bed HTGR, 80 MWe) | Discussions with multiple Canadian utilities | Design review |

<Tip>
The **SMR Action Plan** (2020) endorsed by provinces including Ontario, Saskatchewan, New Brunswick, and Alberta created a policy framework supporting SMR deployment. Engineers interested in cutting-edge nuclear design should follow the CNSC's vendor design review (VDR) process — all major SMR vendors have filed for pre-licensing VDR in Canada.
</Tip>

## Nuclear Safety Regulation — The CNSC

The **Canadian Nuclear Safety Commission (CNSC)** is Canada's independent federal nuclear regulator, established by the *Nuclear Safety and Control Act* (2000). Key characteristics:

- **Independence**: the CNSC operates at arm's length from the government and nuclear industry
- **Licensing**: all nuclear activities in Canada (mining, processing, power generation, waste management, research, transport, import/export) require CNSC licences
- **Regulatory Documents (REGDOCs)**: the CNSC publishes REGDOCs that set specific requirements for licensees — REGDOC-2.5.2 (design of reactor facilities), REGDOC-3.1.1 (reporting), REGDOC-2.7.1 (radiation protection), etc.
- **International engagement**: CNSC collaborates with the IAEA, NEA (OECD Nuclear Energy Agency), and bilateral agreements with the US NRC, UK ONR, etc.

### Radiation Protection Fundamentals

The **ALARA principle** (As Low As Reasonably Achievable) governs all radiation protection programs in Canadian nuclear facilities. CNSC dose limits (whole-body):
- Workers: 50 mSv/year, 100 mSv over 5 years
- Public: 1 mSv/year (above natural background)

## Medical Isotopes — Canada's Life-Saving Nuclear Output

Canada is a world leader in medical isotope production:

- **Cobalt-60 (Co-60)**: produced at CANDU reactors (cobalt rods inserted in reactor) — used in radiation therapy machines (Cobalt-60 teletherapy units) and sterilization of medical equipment. **Best Theratronics** (Ottawa) manufactures radiation therapy equipment.
- **Molybdenum-99 / Technetium-99m**: the most widely used diagnostic medical isotope (~40 million procedures annually worldwide). Produced at the NRU reactor (now shutdown) and increasingly by cyclotrons and linear accelerators. **TRIUMF** (Vancouver) and **Isotope Technologies** play a role in the new distributed production model.
- **Lutetium-177**: emerging therapeutic isotope for prostate and neuroendocrine cancers. **Bruce Power** and **Isogen** (an OPG venture) are building irradiation capacity.
