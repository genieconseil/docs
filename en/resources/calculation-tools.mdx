## Introduction

Choosing the right calculation tool is as important as knowing the underlying engineering method. This page organizes key software by discipline, with special attention to free and open-source options, student access programs, and online calculators. A companion French page covering a wider range of commercial software is available at [/ressources/logiciels-par-discipline](/ressources/logiciels-par-discipline).

<Tip>
The best free tools for engineering students: **Python + SciPy/NumPy** (all disciplines), **HEC-RAS** (hydraulics), **QGIS** (geospatial), **OpenFOAM** (CFD), **LTspice** (circuits), **DWSIM** (chemical processes), **KiCad** (PCB), and **ANSYS Student** (structural/FEA). Learn at least one of these during your studies — they will remain valuable throughout your career.
</Tip>

## Structural Engineering

### Commercial Tools

| Software | Editor | Free Access | Strengths |
|---|---|---|---|
| **ETABS** | Computers & Structures (CSI) | Academic program (ask your dept.) | Multi-story building analysis — industry standard in Canada/US |
| **SAP2000** | Computers & Structures (CSI) | Academic program | General structural analysis — 2D/3D frames, bridges, shells |
| **RISA-3D** | RISA Technologies | Student version available | Steel, concrete, wood buildings |
| **STAAD.Pro** | Bentley Systems | Academic license | International standard, especially in process plants |
| **Revit Structure** | Autodesk | Free via Autodesk Education | BIM-integrated structural modeling |
| **RAM Structural System** | Bentley Systems | Academic license | Integrated building frame design |

### Free and Open-Source Structural Tools

- **OpenSees** (UC Berkeley): open-source finite element framework for earthquake simulation — widely used in seismic research. Python and Tcl interfaces.
- **MASTAN2**: MATLAB-based structural analysis — excellent for learning stiffness method fundamentals. Free for academic use.
- **SkyCiv** (free tier): browser-based structural analysis — beams, trusses, frames. Generous free tier for students.
- **StructX.com**: free online structural calculations — beam formulas, section properties, load tables, steel tables.

### Getting Student Access

**Autodesk Education** program provides free access to Revit, Civil 3D, AutoCAD, and 20+ other products. Register at autodesk.com/education with a school email address. **CSI** (ETABS, SAP2000) has an academic pricing program — contact your university's structural engineering department for group access.

| Software | Free Access | Key Application |

| **SETTLE3 (Rocscience)** | Free Student License | Settlement and consolidation |

### Free Tools

- **Rocscience Student License**: RS2, RS3, SLIDE2, RocData, Dips, Unwedge, RocFall2, Settle3 — all available free for students via rocscience.com/education. This is one of the most generous student programs in engineering software.
- **Python + PLAXIS Remote Scripting**: Plaxis exposes a Python API for batch automation of geotechnical FEM analyses.

## Hydraulics and Hydrology

### Free Government Software (Essential)

These tools are free, well-documented, widely accepted by regulators, and industry standard:

| Software | Source | Application |
|---|---|---|
| **HEC-RAS 6.x** | USACE (US Army Corps of Engineers) | 1D/2D river hydraulics, floodplain mapping, sediment transport, water quality |
| **HEC-HMS** | USACE | Watershed hydrology — rainfall-runoff, hydrograph generation |
| **SWMM 5.2** | US EPA | Urban stormwater — minor/major system design, water quality |
| **HY-8** | FHWA | Culvert hydraulics design |
| **WinTR-55** | USDA NRCS | Small watershed hydrology (Curve Number method) |
| **EPANET 2.2** | US EPA | Water distribution network modeling |

<Info>
**HEC-RAS** is used by virtually every provincial and municipal government in Canada for floodplain delineation. The 2D modeling capabilities added in version 5.0 now allow detailed urban inundation mapping that previously required commercial software. Learning HEC-RAS is time well spent for any civil or environmental engineer.
</Info>

### Commercial Hydraulic Tools

- **InfoWorks ICM** (Autodesk): integrated catchment modeling — the standard for complex sewer-river systems in major Canadian cities
- **Mike FLOOD / Mike URBAN+** (DHI): common in coastal and complex floodplain studies
- **WaterGEMS / WaterCAD** (Bentley): water distribution modeling with GIS integration

## Mechanical Engineering and FEA

### Commercial Tools

| Software | Free Student Access | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **SolidWorks** | Via university license or CSWA program | CSWA/CSWP certification valued by employers |
| **Autodesk Inventor** | Free via Autodesk Education | Alternative to SolidWorks; integrates with AutoCAD |
| **Fusion 360** | Free for students and startups | Cloud-based CAD/CAM/simulation — very accessible entry point |
| **ANSYS Mechanical** | ANSYS Student (free, mesh-limited) | Industry-leading FEA — student version sufficient for coursework |
| **Abaqus/CAE** | SIMULIA Research license (check your university) | Research-grade FEA, nonlinear materials, composites |
| **COMSOL** | University consortium pricing | Multiphysics coupling — excellent for research |

### Free FEA Options

- **FreeCAD + FEM workbench**: open-source CAD with integrated FEA (uses CalculiX solver). Decent for basic stress analysis.
- **CalculiX**: open-source FEA solver with Abaqus-compatible input format. No GUI but highly capable.
- **Code_Aster** (EDF): open-source industrial FEA developed by EDF (France). Used in nuclear applications.
- **PrePoMax**: free GUI front-end for CalculiX — makes open-source FEA accessible without a steep CLI learning curve.

### How to Get SolidWorks Student Access

1. **Via your university**: most engineering schools have SolidWorks network licenses. Ask your lab coordinator.
2. **SolidWorks CSWA exam**: students can purchase a bundled SolidWorks license + CSWA exam voucher (~$60 USD) via the SolidWorks education store.
3. **SOLIDWORKS for Makers**: personal use license for non-commercial projects (~$100 USD/year).

## Chemical Process Engineering

### Free and Open-Source Process Simulation

- **DWSIM** (Daniel Medeiros): the most capable open-source process simulator. Supports VLE calculations, unit operations (distillation, reactors, heat exchangers, separators), and solids processing. Free, cross-platform, active development. Suitable as a learning alternative to Aspen.
- **COCO + ChemSep** (AmsterCHEM): rigorous distillation column simulation — free for academic use. Supports rate-based mass transfer models.
- **Cantera** (Python/MATLAB/C++): open-source chemical kinetics, thermodynamics, and transport library — widely used in combustion research.

### Commercial Tools with Student Access

| Tool | Student Access | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Aspen Plus** | Aspen Academic Program (via university) | Chemical process simulation — reaction + separation + economics |
| **Aspen HYSYS** | Aspen Academic Program (via university) | Oil and gas process simulation — dominant in upstream industry |
| **CHEMCAD** | Student license (~$250/year) | Alternative to Aspen — good value for teaching |
| **MATLAB + Chemical Toolboxes** | Campus license via university | Process control, optimization, data analysis |

<Tip>
The **Aspen Academic** program covers Aspen Plus, HYSYS, Exchanger Design & Rating (EDR), and Aspen Capital Cost Estimator (ACCE). Check if your university's chemical engineering department is enrolled — access is typically available via a campus license for all students. DWSIM is the recommended free alternative for self-study.
</Tip>

## General Engineering Calculation Tools

### MATLAB — The Engineering Standard

MATLAB remains the most widely used numerical computing environment in engineering. Key capabilities:
- Matrix operations, numerical linear algebra
- Signal and image processing toolboxes
- Simulink for dynamic system simulation
- Optimization toolbox (linear programming, nonlinear, global)
- Statistics and Machine Learning toolbox
- Communication with external hardware (Arduino, National Instruments, etc.)

**Student access**: Most Canadian universities provide MATLAB Campus Total licenses — check your IT portal. The student license is typically ~$50/year if not covered by your institution.

### Python — The Free Alternative That Isn't Just an Alternative

Python with scientific libraries has become equally or more capable than MATLAB for most engineering tasks, with the significant advantage of being completely free and having a larger ecosystem.

**Essential libraries for engineers:**

| Library | Purpose |
|---|---|
| **NumPy** | N-dimensional arrays, linear algebra, Fourier transforms |
| **SciPy** | Optimization, integration, interpolation, signal processing, ODE solvers |
| **Pandas** | Data manipulation and analysis — DataFrames, time series |
| **Matplotlib** | Plotting — 2D and basic 3D visualization |
| **Plotly** | Interactive visualization — dashboards, 3D plots |
| **SymPy** | Symbolic mathematics — derivatives, integrals, equation solving |
| **Pint** | Physical units — unit-aware calculations (avoid unit errors) |
| **FEniCS / FEniCSx** | Finite element analysis via Python API |
| **OpenSees (openseespy)** | Structural analysis / earthquake engineering |

**Installation recommendation**: Use **Anaconda** or **Miniconda** for environment management, or **VS Code + Python extension** for a clean development environment.

### Mathcad — Engineering Calculations with Documentation

PTC Mathcad combines calculation with documentation in a math-centric workspace:
- Calculations show symbolically (not just code) — ideal for engineering notes of record
- Full unit tracking — automatically catches unit inconsistencies
- Can reference outputs from Mathcad Prime in company templates

Student access: Mathcad Prime is available as a free trial; many engineering firms use it for formal calculation documentation that must be legible to reviewers and regulators.

### Excel for Engineering

Excel remains ubiquitous in engineering for data analysis, simple calculations, and reporting. Useful add-ins:
- **Solver**: nonlinear optimization — built into Excel
- **@RISK** (Palisade): Monte Carlo simulation add-in
- **Engineering toolboxes**: various third-party add-ins for structural, thermal, and electrical calculations
- **Python via xlwings or openpyxl**: automate Excel workflows from Python scripts

## AI Tools for Engineering Calculations

AI assistants are increasingly used by engineers for:
- **Formula lookup and explanation**: asking Claude, ChatGPT, or Perplexity to explain a formula, its assumptions, and its limitations
- **Code generation**: using GitHub Copilot or Claude to generate Python/MATLAB scripts for standard calculations
- **Unit conversion checks**: quickly verifying dimensional analysis
- **Literature review**: summarizing papers, explaining methods

<Warning>
AI tools can make confident-sounding errors in engineering calculations. They may produce incorrect formulas, miss important safety factors, or apply equations outside their valid range. **Always verify AI-generated calculations against primary references** (textbooks, standards, established calculation tools) before using them in professional work. AI assistants are research and drafting aids — not substitutes for engineering judgment and verified calculation software.
</Warning>

**Appropriate use cases for AI in engineering calculations:**
- Explaining the derivation of a formula you are about to use (then verify the formula independently)
- Generating boilerplate Python code for a standard calculation (then verify the logic)
- Translating between unit systems (then check the result)
- Summarizing a standard or research paper (then read the source)

**Not appropriate**:
- Accepting numerical results from AI without independent verification
- Using AI-generated calculations as the sole basis for a design submitted to an authority
