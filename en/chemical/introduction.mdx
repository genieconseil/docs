## What is Chemical Engineering?

Chemical engineering sits at the productive intersection of chemistry, physics, biology, and engineering. Where a chemist asks *why* a reaction occurs, a chemical engineer asks *how* to make it occur reliably, safely, and economically at industrial scale. The discipline transforms laboratory discoveries into operational processes: a pharmaceutical compound synthesized in milligrams becomes a drug manufactured in metric tonnes; a catalytic reaction observed in a test tube becomes a refinery unit processing thousands of barrels per day.

The defining challenge of chemical engineering is **scale-up**: bridging the gap between bench-scale results and industrial-scale production while managing heat transfer, mass transfer, fluid dynamics, reaction kinetics, and thermodynamics simultaneously. A process that looks clean and simple in a 250 mL flask may become turbulent, exothermic, mass-transfer limited, and economically challenging at 10,000 L.

## Core Competencies

### Mass and Energy Balances

The foundation of every chemical engineering calculation. A mass balance tracks material entering, leaving, accumulating in, or being consumed by a system boundary. An energy balance does the same for enthalpy, internal energy, kinetic energy, and potential energy. Together they determine:

- Flow rates and compositions throughout a process
- Heating and cooling duties at each unit operation
- Reactor conversion and yield constraints
- Process efficiency and utility consumption

**Degree of freedom analysis** determines whether a system of equations is solvable given the available specifications — a skill that transfers directly from single-unit problems to full plant simulations in Aspen Plus or HYSYS.

### Transport Phenomena

Transport phenomena unifies three parallel processes through analogous mathematical frameworks:

| Phenomenon | Driving Force | Key Parameter | Governing Law |
|---|---|---|---|
| Momentum transfer | Pressure gradient / shear stress | Viscosity (μ) | Newton's law of viscosity |
| Heat transfer | Temperature gradient | Thermal conductivity (k) | Fourier's law |
| Mass transfer | Concentration gradient | Diffusivity (D) | Fick's law |

The **Reynolds analogy** and **Colburn analogy** connect these three phenomena, allowing engineers to use experimental heat transfer correlations to estimate mass transfer coefficients — a powerful shortcut in equipment design.

### Reaction Engineering

Chemical reactor design requires quantifying how quickly reactions proceed (kinetics) and how efficiently reactants contact each other (residence time distribution, mixing). The key reactor archetypes are:

- **Batch reactor (BR)**: time-dependent, no flow — pharmaceuticals, specialty chemicals
- **Continuous stirred tank reactor (CSTR)**: steady-state, perfect mixing — polymerization, fermentation
- **Plug flow reactor (PFR)**: steady-state, no axial mixing — gas-phase reactions, tubular reactors

The **Damköhler number** (Da = reaction rate / transport rate) determines whether a reactor is kinetically controlled (Da << 1) or transport-limited (Da >> 1), guiding the focus of scale-up efforts.

### Thermodynamics

Applied thermodynamics in chemical engineering goes beyond the introductory treatment in mechanical engineering. Key topics include:

- **Phase equilibrium**: vapor-liquid equilibrium (VLE), liquid-liquid equilibrium (LLE) — the basis of distillation, extraction, and absorption design
- **Equations of state**: cubic EOS (Peng-Robinson, Soave-Redlich-Kwong) for gas-phase and mixed-phase systems; activity coefficient models (NRTL, UNIQUAC) for liquid mixtures
- **Chemical reaction equilibrium**: Gibbs free energy minimization, equilibrium constants, Le Chatelier's principle applied to reactor conditions
- **Fugacity and activity**: departure from ideal behavior in non-ideal mixtures

### Process Control

A chemical process at steady state is a controlled system: flow controllers maintain material balance, temperature controllers maintain heat balance, and pressure controllers maintain mechanical integrity. Chemical engineers design and tune control loops using:

- **PID control**: proportional-integral-derivative tuning (Ziegler-Nichols, Cohen-Coon methods)
- **HAZOP (Hazard and Operability Study)**: systematic identification of deviations that could cause hazardous conditions
- **Safety instrumented systems (SIS)**: independent protection layers per IEC 61511
- **Advanced process control (APC)**: model predictive control (MPC) for multivariable optimization

## Major Industry Sectors

### Oil & Gas Refining

Canada's oil sands in Alberta represent one of the world's largest hydrocarbon resources. Chemical engineers at companies like **Suncor**, **Cenovus**, and **Imperial Oil** work on:

- **SAGD (Steam Assisted Gravity Drainage)**: injecting steam underground to reduce bitumen viscosity for extraction from oil sands deposits
- **Upgrading**: converting heavy bitumen into synthetic crude oil (SCO) via hydrocracking and coking
- **Amine treating**: removing H₂S and CO₂ from natural gas and refinery off-gases using monoethanolamine (MEA) or other solvents
- **Hydrotreating and hydrocracking**: catalytic reaction with hydrogen to remove sulfur (desulfurization), nitrogen, and metals from crude fractions

Conventional refineries (Ontario, Quebec, BC, Atlantic Canada) operate atmospheric and vacuum distillation, catalytic cracking (FCC), catalytic reforming, alkylation, and blending units to produce transportation fuels, heating oil, and petrochemical feedstocks.

### Petrochemicals

The petrochemical industry converts refinery byproducts (naphtha, ethane, propane) into building-block chemicals:

- **Ethylene** via steam cracking of ethane or naphtha — feedstock for polyethylene, PVC, ethylene oxide, ethylene glycol
- **Propylene** — feedstock for polypropylene, acrylonitrile, propylene oxide
- **Aromatics (BTX — benzene, toluene, xylenes)** — feedstocks for plastics, fibers, solvents, explosives

**NOVA Chemicals** (Corunna, ON and Joffre, AB) is Canada's largest polyethylene producer.

### Pharmaceuticals

Canadian pharmaceutical and biopharmaceutical companies require chemical engineers for:

- **API synthesis**: multi-step organic synthesis of active pharmaceutical ingredients under cGMP (current Good Manufacturing Practices)
- **Bioprocessing**: fermentation and cell culture for biologics (insulin, monoclonal antibodies, vaccines) — sterile design, clean-in-place (CIP), steam-in-place (SIP)
- **Formulation engineering**: solid dosage forms (tablet compression, granulation, coating), injectables, controlled release systems
- **Regulatory compliance**: Health Canada Division 2 (drugs), ICH Q10 (pharmaceutical quality system), ICH Q11 (development of drug substances)

### Food and Beverage

Food engineering applies unit operations to biological materials:

- **Thermal processing**: pasteurization, UHT sterilization, F₀ value calculations for microbial inactivation
- **Evaporation and drying**: juice concentration, milk powder, instant coffee — energy-intensive operations
- **Membrane separation**: ultrafiltration and nanofiltration for dairy (whey protein concentration, milk protein standardization)
- **Fermentation**: beer, wine, yogurt, cheese — microbial growth kinetics and metabolite production

### Water Treatment

Municipal and industrial water treatment applies chemical engineering principles to aqueous systems:

- **Coagulation/flocculation**: alum or ferric sulfate addition to destabilize colloidal particles
- **Filtration**: sand filtration, membrane filtration (MF, UF, NF, RO)
- **Disinfection**: chlorination, UV, ozone — CT (concentration × time) calculations for pathogen inactivation per Health Canada guidelines
- **Wastewater treatment**: biological oxygen demand (BOD) removal via activated sludge, nutrient removal (biological nitrogen and phosphorus removal), biosolids management

### Polymers and Advanced Materials

- **Polymer synthesis**: free radical, ionic, coordination, and step-growth polymerization — controlling molecular weight distribution
- **Composites manufacturing**: resin transfer molding (RTM), prepreg lay-up — interface chemistry and cure kinetics
- **Electrochemical systems**: lithium-ion battery electrode design, fuel cell membrane electrode assemblies — growing rapidly with energy transition

### Mining Processing

Chemical engineers play a central role in mineral processing:

- **Hydrometallurgy**: leaching (acid leach for copper, cyanide leach for gold, pressure oxidation for refractory ores), solvent extraction, electrowinning
- **Pyrometallurgy**: smelting, converting, refining — thermodynamic modeling of high-temperature slag-metal systems
- **Tailings management**: thickening, filtration, paste tailings, tailings storage facility (TSF) design — critical environmental issue

## Canadian Chemical Engineering Landscape

### Alberta — Oil Sands and Petrochemicals

Alberta is the epicenter of Canadian chemical engineering employment. The oil sands complex around Fort McMurray employs thousands of chemical engineers, as do the petrochemical clusters in **Joffre** (NOVA Chemicals polyethylene), **Fort Saskatchewan** (Dow Chemical, Methanex), and **Edmonton** (refineries and upgraders).

### Ontario — Chemicals, Pharmaceuticals, and Manufacturing

Ontario's chemical industry is concentrated in:
- **Sarnia-Lambton "Chemical Valley"**: Imperial Oil, Shell, INEOS, Lanxess — largest concentration of petrochemical facilities in Canada
- **Golden Horseshoe**: Dow Chemical (Ternium), Bayer, BASF, pharmaceutical facilities
- **Ottawa/Montreal corridor**: National Research Council bioprocessing facilities, biopharmaceutical startups

### Quebec — Nutrien, Chemtrade, and Specialty Chemicals

- **Nutrien** (formerly Agrium): largest global potash and nitrogen fertilizer producer — major employer in Saskatchewan and with operations in Quebec
- **Chemtrade Logistics**: sulphuric acid and electrochemical products
- **Tronox**: titanium dioxide from the Sept-Îles ilmenite smelting complex
- Strong food processing sector — dairy, maple products, beverages — in the Laurentians and Eastern Townships
